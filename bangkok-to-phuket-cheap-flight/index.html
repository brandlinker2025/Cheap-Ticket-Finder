Airport warning

BKK and DMK are different airports. BKK is Suvarnabhumi, where international flights from Dhaka normally land. DMK is Don Mueang, across Bangkok. AirAsia itself operates Thai domestic services from both airports (official newsroom source below), Thai Lion Air and Nok Air are DMK-based in our research, and VietJet Thailand is BKK-based. If you land at BKK and your Phuket flight leaves DMK on a separate ticket, you must cross the city — independent guidance in our research suggests keeping at least 3.5–4 hours for a BKK→DMK change, and the safer plan is a BKK departure or a night in Bangkok. That timing is cautious advice, not an official airline rule. Source: AirAsia newsroom — domestic services from both Don Mueang and Suvarnabhumi.

Baggage box

For Nok Air, Thai Lion Air and VietJet Thailand, our research did not verify one cabin/checked number for every fare — confirm the exact kg/pieces and whether checked baggage is included on the booking page for your fare.

Low-cost headline fares often exclude checked baggage. Add bags online first, then compare the final total with bags across airlines and across BKK vs DMK departures.

Separate-ticket & transit/entry caution

Two tickets = two contracts. If the first flight is late, the second airline is not obliged to rebook you free and the second fare can be lost as a no-show. With checked bags on separate tickets you may need immigration → baggage reclaim → check-in again, so keep a large buffer; arriving the night before the domestic leg is our safer advice (guidance, not an official airline rule).

Separate tickets and airport changes can mean entering Thailand. If your Dhaka → Bangkok ticket and this Phuket ticket are separate, or you change between BKK and DMK, do not assume you can stay airside or that any transit/entry arrangement applies to you.

Our research file does not provide a verified official Thai entry link for this page, so we are not guessing one here. Before booking, check your entry/transit requirements on Thailand's official immigration/government sources and with the airline for your exact passport and itinerary — and do not travel on an assumption.

Spell the passenger name exactly as in the passport — low-cost airlines can charge for name correction or refuse boarding (the amount varies, so we do not print a fee here). Cheap low-cost fares are often non-refundable and change fees can be high — read the fare rules before paying. Keep your booking confirmation and PNR safely with you; never send us passport or card images.
